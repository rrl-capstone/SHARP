Care quality in people with and without HIV, aged 50 and older

Komodo Health insurance claims from 2016 to 2025, one year at a time. We find who has HIV, match each person with HIV to a similar person without HIV, compare their chronic conditions and how sick they are, and check how often those conditions get the recommended care.

1Eligible people

A person-year counts when the person is 50 or older on December 31, alive all year, covered for medical and pharmacy claims all year by one payer for each, and has a birth date, sex, state and race on record.

2People with HIV and the control pool

A person has HIV from the first year any of three routes is met: an inpatient HIV diagnosis, two outpatient HIV diagnoses at least seven days apart, or two fills of HIV medicines within 90 days. Medicines that are also used for PrEP, hepatitis B or COVID-19 (lamivudine, ritonavir, tenofovir, emtricitabine, cabotegravir, lenacapavir) do not count on their own. Everyone else who is eligible is in the control pool.

3Conditions before matching

Each person-year is checked for 11 chronic conditions, each counted with at least one inpatient claim or two outpatient claims in the year. Here people with HIV are compared with the whole control pool.

4Propensity score matching

Each year, every person with HIV is matched to one person without HIV of the same sex with the closest propensity score, which comes from birth year, census region and race. A match has to be within 0.2 standard deviations of the score's logit, and no control is used twice in a year.

5Conditions after matching

The same 11 conditions, now in people with HIV and their matched controls, who share their sex and are close in age, region and race.

6How sick: the Gagne score

The Gagne combined comorbidity score adds a weight for each of 20 groups of diagnoses in the year, from −1 for hypertension to 5 for metastatic cancer. HIV/AIDS is one of the groups, with a weight of −1, so the fair comparison leaves it out.

7Care quality

For each condition, the share of person-years in which the person got the recommended care, in the matched groups. Each year is calculated on its own. Comparing within Gagne levels sets people with HIV against controls who are about as sick.

Methods and how to read this site

Three runs

The full cohort is the result. The 1% and 30% samples were used to test the pipeline. Each sample is a fixed set of people chosen by a hash of the patient ID, so the 1% sample is inside the 30% one and the same people are picked every time.

Small numbers are hidden

Only aggregated counts leave the database, and no exact count is published. Any count from 1 to 10 is shown as <11 and the percentages on that row are hidden. Every other count is rounded to the nearest 10 (the smallest shown is 20), and percentages to one decimal, so a hidden count cannot be worked out by subtracting other numbers from a total. Zeros are shown as zeros.

Provisional

The HIV medicine rules, the condition code lists and the care quality codes are still being reviewed with the principal investigator, so treat every number here as provisional.

Matching

The propensity score is a logistic regression of HIV status on birth year, census region and race, fitted once for each year on every person with HIV and 20 randomly chosen controls of the same year and sex. Matching is exact on sex and one to one, nearest neighbour on the logit of the score, highest scores first, without replacement within a year. Balance is the standardized mean difference, scaled by the standard deviation in the HIV group.

Gagne score and care quality

The Gagne score uses Nemo Zhou's ICD-10-CM lookups; a diagnosis counts in the calendar year it falls in. Care quality measures look back further where the measure needs it, for example an eye exam in the last two years.
